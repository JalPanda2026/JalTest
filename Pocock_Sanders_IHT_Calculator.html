      Important information & risks
      • This report is based on current Inheritance Tax legislation as at the date of preparation. Tax rules, rates, reliefs and thresholds (including the Nil Rate Band, Residential Nil Rate Band, Business Relief, and the treatment of pensions from 6 April 2027) are set by HM Government and may change in the future, including with retrospective effect.
      • Levels and bases of taxation, and reliefs from taxation, can change at any time and are not guaranteed. Their value depends on individual circumstances.
      • The figures shown are estimates for illustration purposes only, based on the information and assumptions provided, and should not be relied upon as a precise calculation of any actual tax liability.
      • Taper relief on lifetime gifts is not modelled. Any gifts figure entered reduces the available Nil Rate Band in full. The calculator does not apply the 20%, 40%, 60% or 80% taper reduction that lowers the tax on gifts made three to seven years before death where those gifts exceed the Nil Rate Band, and it does not apply annual or other gift exemptions. The Inheritance Tax position on any gifts must be calculated separately.
      • The reduced 36% charitable rate is applied automatically. Where the charitable legacy entered is 10% or more of the baseline amount, the rate on the remaining taxable estate reduces from 40% to 36%. The baseline amount is the estate after reliefs and the standard nil-rate band, but the residence nil-rate band is excluded from it; the residence nil-rate band still reduces the estate the 36% is charged on. This is modelled as a single estate component and assumes the legacy is funded from the assets entered. Estates with more than one component (for example jointly owned property or settled property passing separately, each with its own 10% test) must be checked separately, as the merger of components to pass a single test is not modelled here.
      • Discounted Gift Trust discount rates are indicative, subject to medical underwriting, and not guaranteed until confirmed by the provider.
      • Business Relief (BPR)-qualifying investments carry investment risk, including potential loss of capital, are not capital guaranteed, and relief is only secured once the qualifying holding period (normally two years) has been met and the shares are still held on death. Relief can also be withdrawn if the underlying company ceases to qualify. Since 6 April 2026, 100% relief on combined BPR/APR-qualifying property (including any existing holdings) is capped at £2.5 million per person, with 50% relief (an effective 20% Inheritance Tax rate) on any value above that. AIM-listed shares are treated differently from wider Business Relief holdings and do not draw on the £2.5 million allowance: since 6 April 2026 they attract 50% relief only (an effective 20% Inheritance Tax rate) regardless of amount, rather than the 100% relief previously available.
      • Assets placed into trust (including a Discounted Gift Trust) are no longer accessible to the settlor(s) beyond any retained rights specifically reserved, and trust legislation itself is subject to change.
      • Whole of Life insurance premiums are not guaranteed for the life of the policy unless stated as guaranteed by the provider, and cover may lapse if premiums are not maintained.
